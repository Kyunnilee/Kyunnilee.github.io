<div class="tldr">
  <b>TL;DR</b>
  <p>We build <b>APRO</b>, a simulator benchmark that tests whether VLMs can <b>use physical reasoning to act</b>, not just answer questions about physics.</p>
  <p>480 items, 11 tasks, 3 regimes: <b>infer</b> properties, <b>predict</b> from past trials, and <b>adapt</b> through interaction.</p>
  <p>Models get better with evidence, but <b>turning that into successful robot actions is still hard</b>.</p>

## Motivation

A robot picks up a hammer and it tilts. That tilt tells it where the mass is, so it should grasp somewhere else before swinging.

Existing benchmarks test either **physics understanding** (fixed images, no action) or **task success** (no view into why it failed). We want both, in one loop.

## Three regimes

- **R1 · Infer** — predict a physical property from one observation.
- **R2 · Predict from experience** — see earlier trials, then predict a new one.
- **R3 · Adapt** — act, get feedback, try again until the goal is reached.

<div class="note">
  <b>Oracle setting.</b> We can also hand the model ground-truth simulator values (mass, friction, …), step by step. This separates "didn't know the physics" from "couldn't use it".
</div>

## Takeaways

1. Models can infer physics, but struggle to **act** on it.
2. Past trials help most at the **start**.
3. Giving the true physics helps prediction, but **not always manipulation** — execution is the bottleneck.

<div class="note">
  <b>Limitations.</b> Simulation only; few tool tasks; no human baseline.
</div>
