  <p>
  We build <span class="apro-name">APRO (Agentic Physical Reasoning Olympiad)</span>, a simulation-grounded evaluation suite to measure agentic physical reasoning. We test if VLMs can infer physical properties, use observations from given trials, and actively to acquire informative evidence.
  </p>
  <b>We find that models are capable on acting when they have the correct evidence, yet, they struggle to exploring to acquire it.</b>

  The key takeaways are:
  <ul>
  <li><b>Models know how to use evidence to improve physical perception (R1).</b></li>
  <li><b>Models know how to use evidence from given trials, yet they get confused in more complex setups (R2).</b></li>
  <li><b>Models struggle to generate good actions that helps to acquire information (R3).</b></li>
  </ul>

## Agentic Physical Reasoning

My research has always played with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), when I first met them, they were just a simple "chat bot" as visual assistants: I would use to test image captioning, VQA skills or simply test if they understand images. And now begining my PhD, I'm very excited to see how much progress they have been made, where we have to call them real agents.

VLMs can now reason about physical environments, plan actions, and even execute them through robotics control<sup class="apro-cite" id="cite-1"><a href="#ref-openai2026gpt61sol">1</a>–<a href="#ref-meta2026musespark13">16</a></sup>. I've been recently seeing very exciting demos on Twitter that shows this progress. This excites me showing a new direction in robotics, where <b>VLMs can serve as both high-level planners<sup class="apro-cite" id="cite-2"><a href="#ref-driess2023palm">17</a>,<a href="#ref-shi2025hi">18</a></sup> and low-level executors<sup class="apro-cite" id="cite-3"><a href="#ref-brohan2023rt">19</a>–<a href="#ref-black2024pi_0">21</a></sup></b>.

But despite this excitement, I havent met studies yet that diagnostically studies on <b>how useful VLMs are in controlling robots</b>. So I wanted to ask this question starting my first project in Berkeley: <it>Can these models infer physical properties from what they see? Can they learn from what happened in earlier attempts? And can they act, watch the result, and fix their actions?</it>

In closed loop settings, VLMs choose and adjust actions based on feedback. In the physical world world, physical reasoning<sup class="apro-cite" id="cite-4"><a href="#ref-li2024phyre">22</a>–<a href="#ref-sun2026triphysbench">25</a></sup> plays an important role here, where <b>physical properties of the scene often determines the outcome of actions</b>.

Look at the two videos below, where the two robots are given the same task "Use a hammer to pull out a nail". Both initially grasp the hammer in a way that causes it to tilt, but the outcome differs on how they refine their answers. The success case shows that the model <b>observes the instability</b> and <b>adjusts its grasp</b> closer to the hammer head. The failure case on the other hand continues with the unstable grasp and eventually fails. We can obviously get the fact that understanding the physical world requires not only observation, but also <b>actively acquiring physical information and using it to guide our actions</b>.

To study this, we carefully created (1) <b>three evaluation regimes</b> to test this,(2) generated data in simulation for a controlled setting (<span class="apro-name">APRO</span> dataset), and (3)conducted a <b>Oracle setting evaluation and analysis</b> (Oracle settings) to disentagle how useful VLMs are in controling robots as agents in the physical world.

## Three evaluation regimes

<a href="{{ '/assets/img/blog/apro/fig1_regimes.svg' | relative_url }}" target="_blank" title="Open full size"><img class="regime-fig" src="{{ '/assets/img/blog/apro/fig1_regimes.svg' | relative_url }}" alt="Three evaluation regimes of APRO"></a>

<div class="caption">Three Evaluation Regimes for <span class="apro-name">APRO</span>.</div>

The simplest way to understand physical perception to action,

- <b>R1</b>: Can the model understand and predict physics?
- <b>R2</b>: Can the model learn from previous observations?
- <b>R3</b>: Can the model actively interact with the physical world and accomplish a task?

The three regimes represent increasing levels of interaction and agency.

We generated a dataset, <span class="apro-name">APRO</span> (Agentic Physical Reasoning Olympiad), simulator grounded<sup class="apro-cite" id="cite-5"><a href="#ref-todorov2012mujoco">26</a>–<a href="#ref-li2024behavior">28</a></sup>, we organized the tasks under the three evaluation regimes mentioned above.

### Oracle settings

Why do models fail? <b>Lack of information</b> they got from the world? or is it because they <b>cannot reason with it</b>?

  <div class="card-a">
    <h4><i class="fa-solid fa-brain"></i>Limited reasoning</h4>
    When you have the true value to solve a high level task, can you use that information during reasoning to improve?
  </div>

<details class="setup">
  <summary>In the real world we dont get parameter values?: We have sensors, we get to know why they fail</summary>
  Consider a robot using a Sharpa hand with tactile sensors to manipulate an object. As the robot touches and grasps the object, its sensors continuously provide information about contact, force, pressure. <b>Given these parameter signals</b> from the sensors, <b>can robots use this information well to manipulate correctly during action?</b>

Here is one more example: In autonomous driving, when a car approaches another vehicle, its sensors provide information such as the distance to the front car, the speed of both vehicles, and their relative velocity. <b>These physical values are critical for deciding</b> whether the car should accelerate, slow down, or maintain its speed. As the environment changes, the vehicle continuously receives updated information and adjusts its actions accordingly.
</details>

To tell these apart, we run an <b>oracle analysis</b>: we give the model the true values from the simulator, one group at a time, and check what changes.

### Regime 3 Results

<div class="finding">
  <span class="exp-num">Finding 4</span>
  <p><b>Models struggle to generate good actions that helps to acquire information.</b> Without the oracle settings, models only partly succeed in R3 tasks. With information given to reason with(Oracle setting), they can solve more scenes. This indicates action generation to acquire this information is the bottleneck.</p>
</div>


As in Regime 3 task, the VLM has to generate actions to acquire information themselves and perceive physical properties from it. Although models do improve given more trials, when they explicitly have the information improves the models easier.
How much models can improve with oracle settings indicate utilization of information is doable, yet, **models struggle to explore with their own actions to generate better actions.**
